Medical Sensors and Therapeutic Delivery Engineering Laboratory

Small materials that sense, carry and treat.

The SenThera Laboratory designs nanoscale biosensors and targeted therapeutic systems: tools that find disease early and deliver treatment only where it is needed.

Research

Three connected areas

The lab works across the full path from material design to biological function. Sensing, nanomaterials and targeted therapy share the same core question: how to make a material recognize one molecule, one microbe or one cell type among thousands.

01 · SENSING

Biosensors and diagnostic platforms

Electrochemical and optical sensors that detect proteins, nucleic acids, pathogens, drugs and cancer biomarkers quickly, at low concentration, and outside the central lab.

  • Electrochemical
  • Optical & fluorescent
  • Surface plasmon resonance
  • Microfluidic
  • Point-of-care & portable
  • Aptasensors & immunosensors
  • Machine learning readout
Bare electrodeBioreceptor layer+ Target bound

02 · NANOTECHNOLOGY

Nanomaterial design and characterization

Synthesis, surface engineering and characterization of nanoparticles and nanostructures, with control of size, charge, morphology, loading and release.

  • Carbon nanotubes
  • Liposomes & micelles
  • Polymeric nanoparticles
  • Mesoporous silica
  • Magnetic nanoparticles
  • Nanofibers
  • Hydrogels
  • Nanoemulsions
03 · TARGETED THERAPEUTICS

Targeted and controlled-release therapy

Carriers that recognize diseased cells and release drugs or radiotherapy at the site, to raise efficacy and reduce side effects. Includes theranostic systems that treat and report at the same time.

  • Ligand-targeted nanoparticles
  • Antibody–drug conjugates
  • Peptide-targeted carriers
  • Aptamer–drug conjugates
  • Stimuli-responsive release
  • Radiopharmaceuticals
  • Theranostics
FLUORESCENCE · CELL UPTAKE
Research highlight

A CD19-targeted radiopharmaceutical for B-cell lymphomas and leukemias

A precision delivery platform that pairs molecular targeting with the potency of radiotherapy.

  • Characterizing the biophysical and structural properties of the CD19-binding ligand to optimize stability and binding.
  • Radiolabeling the targeting ligand and evaluating its tumor-targeting capability in cancer models.
